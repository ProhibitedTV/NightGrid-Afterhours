# Cascadia — regional vision

Bay Area precedent: [MTC regional structure](https://mtc.ca.gov/about-mtc/what-mtc), [bridges](https://www.mtc.ca.gov/operations/programs-projects/bridges), [Oakland geography](https://www.oaklandca.gov/Planning-Building/General-Plan-Neighborhood-Plans/City-of-Oakland-Current-General-Plan-Elements/Oakland-2045-General-Plan-Update/GPU-Options-for-How-We-Stabilize-and-Grow/Options-Chapter-1), [Carquinez industrial history](https://www.spur.org/publications/urbanist-article/2012-06-03/cities-carquinez). Fictional original geography; no copied street data. Cascadia is the approved fictional state name; all other new names and boundaries remain provisional. This is alternate-history fiction, not an endorsement or representation of any real-world independence or statehood proposal.

Morrow Bay remains the playable starting city and primary quality target. Ironhaven (industrial East Bay) and Silica Valley (South Bay technology center) are planned major cities. Four smaller settlements and landscapes provide transition rather than continuous high-density city. Regional atlas is schematic only.

## Longer-term horizon
After the initial Bay-inspired region is functioning, consider separate rainy river and northern port metropolises inspired by Portland and Seattle, with evergreen wilderness, mountain roads and hydroelectric infrastructure. No new city names, coordinates, travel distances or current production allocation are implied. The regional atlas describes the initial metropolitan region, not the complete state.
