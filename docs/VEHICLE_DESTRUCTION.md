# Vehicle destruction — first production pass

The Cinder R9 uses existing physical collision closing speed, four persisted damage zones, damaged lamps, body shifts, side damage and reduced power. This pass adds true vertex-level end crumpling of the original mesh, including separate head and tail optics, without mutating shared imported assets. Geometry remains bounded to the outermost 0.54 m of the ends (up to 24 cm longitudinal plus small buckles); collision remains conservative. The mesh rebuilds only when a damage zone changes. Repair restores the exact pristine source. At the existing minimum condition of 18, occupied cars lose drive power and brake to a stop; exit and repair remain available. The four-channel optional damage field is now written for damaged Kestrels too; pristine sedan records stay unchanged, and missing legacy damage loads pristine geometry.

This is arcade destruction, not soft-body physics. No detached doors, explosion, shattered glass, wreck smoke, NPC fleet destruction or dynamic collision hull resizing yet. The Kestrel now receives impact damage, deforms its front monocoque and selected authored rear parts, moves front hardware with the crush field, and loses head/rear lamps at high damage. The same visual rig is shared with police cars; NPC traffic buses/sedans are not covered. Test the native result with F10 before promoting visual quality. Verify impacts at different speeds, rear-end and head-on hits, glancing contacts, damage restore, repair and blocked exit. No claim of native FPS or crash realism until measured.

The headless regression exercises real CharacterBody3D wall contact, localized geometry, unchanged collision, original resource isolation and repair, and power loss. Next passes: per-side local dents, loose panels with capped rigid-body debris, damaged wheel alignment, visual smoke, NPC traffic integration; add native before/after captures.
