# Vehicle destruction — first production pass

The Cinder R9 uses existing physical collision closing speed, four persisted damage zones, damaged lamps, body shifts, side damage and reduced power. This pass adds true vertex-level end crumpling of the original mesh, including separate head and tail optics, without mutating shared imported assets. Geometry remains bounded to the outermost 0.54 m of the ends (up to 24 cm longitudinal plus small buckles); collision remains conservative. The mesh rebuilds only when a damage zone changes. Repair restores the exact pristine source. At the existing minimum condition of 18, occupied cars lose drive power and brake to a stop; exit and repair remain available. The four-channel optional damage field is now written for damaged Kestrels too; pristine sedan records stay unchanged, and missing legacy damage loads pristine geometry.

This is arcade destruction, not soft-body physics. Severe side damage now detaches the Cinder's actual articulated door; severe front damage detaches the Kestrel's original front splitter. Their real meshes and finishes become capped, short-lived rigid debris. Detached visibility derives from saved damage, no debris is spawned during restore, and repair returns original panels. Conservative original vehicle hull and safe exit checks stay intact. No other detachable panels, explosion, fully shattered/removable glass, permanent NPC wrecks or dynamic collision hull resizing yet. Civilian sedans and buses now receive collisions, crumple at their front/rear ends, temporarily stop and resume at reduced speed. Fixed fleet crash state round-trips in an optional save field; ambient pooled cars reset on retirement/reuse. Existing wheels remain batched. Severe crashes now generate short-lived rigid metal fragments (maximum 18 world-wide, no vehicle-blocking collision layer, seven-second expiry). Damaged cars emit localized gray smoke (maximum six emitters, 22 particles each, 44 m visibility). Side damage produces small visual wheel camber and a steering pull on both player vehicles. Repair and restore clear transient debris; smoke and alignment reconstruct from condition/zones. The Kestrel now receives impact damage, deforms its front monocoque and selected authored rear parts, moves front hardware with the crush field, and loses head/rear lamps at high damage. The same visual rig is shared with police cars; Autonomous traffic now has its own bounded damage path. Side impacts on NPCs are approximated by the nearest end; bus route and boarding policy remain intact. Test the native result with F10 before promoting visual quality. Verify impacts at different speeds, rear-end and head-on hits, glancing contacts, damage restore, repair and blocked exit. No claim of native FPS or crash realism until measured.

Severe impact events now emit short-lived directional sparks (four bursts globally, 18 particles each, no extra dynamic light); Cinder laminated glass shows procedural crack lines driven by the saved damage, not shattered glass physics. Native crack appearance and particle quality are not yet approved.

The headless regression exercises real CharacterBody3D wall contact, localized geometry, unchanged collision, original resource isolation and repair, and power loss. Next passes: per-side local dents, additional detachable panels, localized side mesh dents, fully shattered glass, native crash captures; add native before/after captures.
